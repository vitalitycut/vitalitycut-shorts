## Do more of
No proven winners yet, so keep the starting guidance and keep the grid balanced:
- Topics tied to a fresh study or headline, in broad-appeal areas: supplements people already buy, sleep, everyday food and drink (coffee, tea, juice, sweeteners), time-saving fitness, brain/phone habits.
- Hooks of 8 words or fewer that challenge a belief, name a number, or name something the viewer owns, eats or does.
- The "Reality check" beat in every video.
- Keep spreading length across 24–45s and hooks across question / statement / number, so each group stays at 5+ videos.

## Do less of
- Don't change channel direction on no data. No topic area is "banned" for performance reasons yet.
- Don't pile up "Your ___" hooks: 5 so far; alternate with other second-person forms ("Do you…", "If you…") so the hook test stays readable.
- 08:00 has the fewest videos (4 live vs 7 each at 12:00 and 18:00). Keep the 08:00 slot filled every day so it catches up.
- Don't repeat a format within the same day (every day so far has kept to this; keep it).

## Format mix
Used so far: quick explainer (4 incl. today), myth vs fact (4), X vs Y (4), 3 things (4), what happens when (3), explainer + easy wins (1). Next 3 days: favor **what happens when** and **explainer + easy wins** to even them up. Never the same format two videos in a row and never twice in one day.

## Keep it monetizable
Since July 16, 2026, YouTube doesn't monetize repetitive or templated content with little variation between videos ("inauthentic content"). Every video must look and read different from the last one:
- Change the palette, particle style, tag line, label wording and beat structure each time (beat count has been 6–8; keep using all three).
- Vary length between about 24 and 45 seconds.
- Make each one teach something specific; never pad with generic lines.
- Owner voiceover videos (from `voice/`) add a human, non-templated element; use any that are available first. None have been recorded yet, which is the single biggest anti-template safeguard still unused.
- At 3 a day, the risk of looking mass-produced is higher. Same-day videos must differ in format, hook style, palette and tag line. Recent "The study" / "Reality check" label pairs repeat in most videos; vary the middle labels' wording more.
