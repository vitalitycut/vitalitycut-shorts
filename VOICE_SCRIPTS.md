# Voiceover scripts

Record any script below on your phone (Voice Memos is fine), then upload it to the `voice/` folder named with its slug, e.g. `morning-light.m4a`. The autopilot turns each recording into a Short on its next run. Newest scripts are at the top.

Tips: record in a quiet room with the phone about 6 inches from your mouth. Talk like you're telling a friend, a little faster and more energetic than normal. Retakes are fine; only upload the take you like.

## exercise-nox4
**Topic:** A muscle protein that fades with age, and what exercise did to it in mice · **Length:** about 20 seconds

> Here's one more reason to keep moving as you get older. Monash University researchers found a muscle protein called NOX4 drops with age and inactivity, and low levels went with smaller, weaker muscles. In older mice, exercise helped bring it back. This is early animal research, not proof for people. Take a walk today, and follow for more.

**Sources:** Tiganis et al. (Monash Biomedicine Discovery Institute), Science Advances 2026, via News-Medical (June 11, 2026) and Fox News Health (June 14, 2026).

## mind-diet-aging-clock
**Topic:** The MIND diet, pace of aging and dementia risk · **Length:** about 20 seconds

> Can your plate slow your aging clock? In the Framingham Heart Study, about sixteen hundred adults over sixty who stuck closer to the MIND diet, think vegetables, berries, nuts, whole grains, olive oil and fish, aged more slowly on a DNA-based aging clock and had lower dementia risk. It's a link, not proof. Try one MIND-style meal this week, and follow for more.

**Sources:** "Diet, Pace of Biological Aging, and Risk of Dementia in the Framingham Heart Study," Annals of Neurology 2024 (1,644 adults 60+), via Columbia Mailman School of Public Health (Mar 14, 2024) and PsyPost (Mar 18, 2024).

## daylight-dementia
**Topic:** Bright daytime light and dementia risk · **Length:** about 20 seconds

> Here's a reason to step outside today. In a study of nearly eighty-eight thousand adults who wore light sensors on their wrists, people who got brighter daytime light had about a sixteen percent lower dementia risk over roughly eight years. It's a link, not proof that light protects your brain. Take your coffee outside, and follow for more.

**Sources:** Zheng, Feng et al. (Guangzhou Medical University), General Psychiatry 2026 (DOI 10.1002/gps3.70039), UK Biobank cohort of 87,577 adults, via ScienceDaily / Wiley (Oct 29, 2026) and News-Medical (June 29, 2026).

## steps-focus
**Topic:** Daily steps and focus later in life · **Length:** about 20 seconds

> Want a sharper brain later on? Count your steps. Researchers looked at Fitbit data from about three thousand adults over fourteen years. People averaging around nine thousand steps a day or more scored better on focus and self-control tests than people around five thousand. It's a link, not proof. Add one short walk today, and follow for more.

**Sources:** Scientific Reports 2026, Fitbit data from 3,093 adults in the NIH All of Us Research Program, via Medical News Today (Oct 29, 2026) and ARY News (Oct 21, 2026).

## four-minute-strength
**Topic:** A 4-minute daily strength routine for adults over 65 · **Length:** about 20 seconds

> Four minutes a day. That's it. In a Penn State trial, adults over sixty-five did push-ups, chair stands, band rows and step-ups, thirty seconds each, most days for twelve weeks. They got stronger at standing up and better at balancing on one leg. It was one small trial, so results may vary. Send this to someone you love, and follow for more.

**Sources:** Sciamanna, Dandekar et al. (Penn State College of Medicine), PLOS One 2026 (DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0336748), randomized trial of 97 adults over 65, via News-Medical (June 10, 2026) and ScienceDaily (Oct 21, 2026).

## three-social-things
**Topic:** Social activities and thinking skills with age · **Length:** about 20 seconds

> Want a sharper brain later in life? Get a busier calendar. A study of almost fifty-eight thousand older adults in twenty-four countries found people doing three or more social activities scored best on memory and thinking tests. Volunteering and sports clubs stood out. It's a link, not proof. Pick one, and follow for more.

**Sources:** "Association between social activity and cognitive enhancement among older adults: results from five longitudinal cohort studies," Journal of Epidemiology & Community Health 2026 (DOI 10.1136/jech-2025-225670), via BMJ Group press release (Oct 20, 2026) and Medical News Today (Oct 27, 2026).

## fiber-calories
**Topic:** High-fiber eating and how many calories you absorb · **Length:** about 20 seconds

> Same plate size, fewer calories? In an Arizona State study, adults on a high-fiber diet absorbed about a hundred and sixteen fewer calories a day than on a Western-style diet, and they didn't feel hungrier. The researchers think gut microbes took a share of that energy. It was a controlled diet study, so everyday life may differ. Follow for more.

**Sources:** Davis, Krajmalnik-Brown, Rittmann et al. (Arizona State University, AdventHealth Translational Research Institute), PLOS One 2026 (DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0347668), via ScienceDaily (Oct 12, 2026; story source: Arizona State University).

## breakfast-stress
**Topic:** Everyday habits linked to handling stress · **Length:** about 20 seconds

> Want to handle stress better? Start with breakfast. A Binghamton University survey of about four hundred college students found that regular breakfast, twenty-plus minutes of exercise, and more than six hours of sleep were all linked to bouncing back from stress better. It's a survey, so it shows a link, not proof. Eat, move, sleep, and follow for more.

**Sources:** Begdache, Cherry & Talkachov (Binghamton University), Journal of American College Health 2026, via Binghamton News (Mar 17, 2026) and ScienceDaily (Oct 13, 2026).

## mediterranean-microproteins
**Topic:** Mediterranean diet and tiny "protective" proteins · **Length:** about 20 seconds

> Olive oil, fish and beans might be doing something sneaky inside your cells. USC researchers found older adults who ate the most Mediterranean-style had higher levels of tiny proteins called humanin and SHMOOSE, plus less oxidative stress. But it was a small observational study, so it can't prove the food caused it. Pass the olive oil, and follow for more.

**Sources:** Vicinanza, Cohen et al. (USC Leonard Davis School of Gerontology), Frontiers in Nutrition 2026, via ScienceDaily (Oct 24, 2026) and USC Leonard Davis news.

## phone-fomo
**Topic:** What drives problem phone use · **Length:** about 20 seconds

> Think you're addicted to your phone because of your personality? Maybe not. A Semmelweis University study of college students found personality alone didn't predict problem phone use. Weak self-control and fear of missing out did. The heaviest scrollers also did worse on memory and attention tests. It's a link, not proof. Try one notification-free hour today, and follow for more.

**Sources:** Takács & Seregély (Semmelweis University), Acta Psychologica 2025, via ScienceDaily (Oct 12, 2026) and The Star / Tribune News Service (Oct 27, 2026).

## ten-minutes-sleep
**Topic:** Daily movement and deeper sleep · **Length:** about 20 seconds

> Want deeper sleep? Try moving a little every day. A UT Austin study tracked students wearing Fitbits for months. On average, the ones who exercised more often got more deep sleep, and as little as ten minutes of breathing-harder activity a day seemed to help. It's a link in young adults, not proof. Go move, and follow for more.

**Sources:** Corral, Baird et al. (UT Austin), Journal of Physical Activity & Health 2025, observational Fitbit study of UT students, via UT News (July 2025) and ScienceDaily (Oct 2026).

## olive-oil-first
**Topic:** Olive oil before carbs and blood sugar · **Length:** about 20 seconds

> Does the order you eat food matter? In a Kyoto University study, mice that got olive oil before sugar had a smaller early blood sugar spike than mice that got it after. The fat slowed the stomach down and boosted gut hormones. But this was in mice, and human trials still need to happen. Follow for more.

**Sources:** Yabe et al. (Kyoto University), Diabetes 2026, mouse study, via Kyoto University research news (Oct 9, 2026) and ScienceDaily (Oct 11, 2026).

## four-minute-workout
**Topic:** A 4-minute daily strength routine for people over 65 · **Length:** about 20 seconds

> Got four minutes? A Penn State trial gave adults over 65 a daily routine: push-ups, chair stands, band rows and step-ups, thirty seconds each with rests in between. After about twelve weeks, they stood up from a chair faster and balanced longer. The gains were modest, and it was under a hundred people. Start easy, and follow for more.

**Sources:** Sciamanna et al. (Penn State College of Medicine), PLOS One 2026, randomized trial of 97 adults 65+ (FAST-2), via Penn State News and ScienceDaily (Oct 21, 2026).

## late-night-eating
**Topic:** Late-night eating and liver health · **Length:** about 20 seconds

> Do you snack right before bed? A study of nearly two hundred thousand people found late-night eaters had about twenty to thirty percent higher rates of liver disease. That's a link, not proof, and eating times were self-reported. Researchers are now testing whether eating earlier actually helps. Maybe close the kitchen a little earlier. Follow for more.

**Sources:** Wilechansky et al. (OHSU, Mass General), Clinical Gastroenterology and Hepatology 2026, observational analysis of the Nurses' Health Studies and Health Professionals Follow-up Study, via ScienceDaily (Oct 15, 2026) and News-Medical (Oct 9, 2026).

## cyclic-sighing
**Topic:** A 5-minute breathing exercise for stress · **Length:** about 20 seconds

> Stressed? Try this five-minute breathing trick. Breathe in through your nose, add a second short sip of air, then exhale slowly through your mouth. In a Stanford study, five minutes a day for a month boosted mood a bit more than mindfulness. It was small and in healthy adults, so it's no cure. Try it tonight, and follow for more.

**Sources:** Spiegel, Huberman et al. (Stanford Medicine), Cell Reports Medicine, Jan 2023 (111 healthy adults, 1 month), via Stanford Medicine news and Greater Good Science Center (UC Berkeley).

## light-weights
**Topic:** Light weights can build muscle · **Length:** about 20 seconds

> Think you need heavy weights to build muscle? Not necessarily. Research suggests lighter weights can build similar muscle, as long as you push each set close to the point where you can't do another rep. That effort is what does the work. Not a shortcut, though: those last few reps should feel really hard. Follow for more.

**Sources:** Newcastle University HNERC blog (Lim, Sept 2024), citing Schoenfeld et al. 2017 and Carvalho et al. 2022 meta-analyses; Christopher Hurst (Newcastle University) in The Conversation, republished by ScienceDaily, Oct 2026.

## morning-light
**Topic:** Morning daylight and sleep · **Length:** about 20 seconds

> Can't fall asleep at night? Try fixing your mornings. Getting outside in daylight soon after you wake up helps set your body clock, so you feel sleepy at the right time later. Even a few minutes counts, and longer on cloudy days. It won't cure insomnia, but it's free. Try it tomorrow and follow for more.

**Sources:** Research on light and the circadian system (e.g. Blume et al., 2019, "Effects of light on human circadian rhythms, sleep and mood," Somnologie); Sleep Foundation guidance on light exposure.
